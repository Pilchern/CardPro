# CardPro audit — September 2026

A full-stack audit of the repository as of commit `3dabbfa`, measured
against the live corpus in `data/ebay_alert_price_history.json` (5,277
distinct listings, 22 calendar dates, 2026-08-21 .. 2026-09-16) rather than
argued from invented examples. Where a claim here has a number attached, the
number came from running the code over that corpus.

**Headline:** the architecture, the observability discipline and the
report/renderer split are in good shape and did not need rework. The
valuation engine had one class of defect left, and it was the same class the
2.0 pass was built to remove — *comp buckets that hold more than one card* —
arriving through three doors `market_key` does not cover. Sixteen of the
engine's would-be flags on the live corpus were replayed; **eight were
artifacts of a bucket holding a different card.** All eight are fixed.

Two scraping defects were found by running the parser rather than reading
it, and one of them writes a wrong number into the 180-day comp corpus.

---

## CRITICAL

### C1. Autographs, relics and print runs were invisible to the comp key — FIXED
**Where:** `src/comps.py` `_key_exact` / `_key_same_card`

`exact` and `same_card` keyed on player, year, set, parallel, card number
and market. A base *2026 Topps Chrome Logofractor* shares every one of those
with an **on-card autograph** of it, and with a **/50 parallel** of it.

**Measured:** 18 of the 108 `same_card` buckets deep enough to be used
(17%) mixed autographed with unautographed copies; 33 (31%) mixed print
runs. Concretely, one bucket held:

```
  $  0.99  Logofractor Rookie Autographs RC SP
  $  4.00  Logofractor RC #259            <- base card
  $  4.30  Logofractor RC #259            <- base card
  $ 59.99  LogoFractor RC Auto #RA-CM
  $109.67  LOGOFRACTOR ON CARD ROOKIE Auto /75
  $125.00  Logofractor Rookie Auto /150
  $155.00  LogoFractor RC Auto /150 RA-CM
  $225.00  Logofractor Auto RA-CM Gold #26/50
```

Weighted median $109.67, so both **base cards were reported as 96% under
market**. `is_autograph` and `print_run` were being extracted and stored the
whole time; nothing keyed on them.

**Why it matters:** this is the exact failure the 2.0 rebuild exists to
prevent (`docs/CARDPRO_2_AUDIT.md` failure mode #3), and it reached the
`same_card` level, which *is* allowed to declare a deal.

**Fix:** `comps.printing_variant()` segments every flag-eligible bucket on
`(autograph, relic, numbering)`. A truncated title refuses the level on the
**recording** side (a hidden "Auto /150" raises a base bucket's median —
the expensive direction) and is allowed on the **lookup** side (a hidden
autograph compared against base copies understates its own discount — the
direction that errs in the user's favour, and the report already prints
"eBay truncated the title" as a risk on those). Refusing both sides would
have cost a third of all valuations to prevent an error that cannot mislead.

### C2. `same_card` pooled inserts with the base card — FIXED
**Where:** `src/comps.py` `_key_same_card`

`same_card` omits the card number on purpose, so that nine copies of #BD-72
plus one listing whose number could not be read stay one bucket. The cost is
that an **insert** — same player, same year, same product, same parallel,
different card, different number — lands in it too.

**Measured:**

```
  $ 3.99  2024 Topps Chrome - Pete Crow-Armstrong #16 Refractor (RC)
  $16.99  ... 89CB-19 35th Anniversary Refractor
  $18.40  ... 1989 REFRACTOR Rc. #89CB-19        <- insert
  $54.00  ... #16 Refractor Rookie RC            <- base
  $55.00  ... #16 Refractor (RC)
  $59.90  ... #16 Prism Refractor (RC)
  $69.95  ... #16 Refractor MINT CENTERED
```

The insert was reported as **69% under market**.

**Fix:** a `mixed_card_numbers` gate. The level still answers, as context,
but may not declare a deal when the bucket holds two known card numbers, or
when the listing's own number is not the bucket's. The second half only
works from the lookup: self-exclusion removes the insert and leaves the
bucket looking perfectly clean to the one listing it is about to misvalue.

### C3. A shipping cost could be read as the card's asking price — FIXED
**Where:** `src/ebay_email_alerts.py` `_extract_price`

`_extract_price` took the first `$N` in a chunk of text, and `_find_nearby`
walks the scoped chunks nearest-first. A sibling cell holding only
`+$4.99 shipping` handed 4.99 back as the price.

**Measured directly:** `_extract_price("+ $4.99 shipping") == 4.99`.

**Why it matters:** this is not a missing price, it is a confident wrong
one, and it lands in two places at once — a $120 card at $4.99 is a
96%-under-market "deal" **and** an asking price sitting in the 180-day comp
corpus that no card was ever listed at, misvaluing that card for six months.

**Fix:** both extractors now share one rule about which money amount is
postage. As a side-effect shipping is now read from `Shipping: $4.99`, a
spelling `SHIPPING_RE` never matched.

### C4. The same item arriving in two alert emails became two listings — FIXED
**Where:** `src/ebay_email_alerts.py` `fetch_alert_listings`

Deduping happened inside one email; across emails the lists were
concatenated. eBay sends one alert per saved search, so a card matching two
searches arrives twice, and `lookback_days: 2` overlaps consecutive runs on
top of that.

**Why it matters:** two identity extractions, two comp lookups, and two rows
in every `RunStats` counter — so the SYSTEM HEALTH footer over-counted the
day it exists to describe. `report.classify_sections` and `focus.select`
both carry comments working around this downstream; the duplicate was never
removed at the source.

**Fix:** merged at the source with the rule already written for two anchors
in one email — fullest title wins, each field filled by whichever copy knew
it. This also *recovers* data: a price from one email and a shipping cost
from another now land on one listing.

## HIGH

### H1. Unreadable buying format fails open into the asking-price corpus — MITIGATED
**Where:** `src/main.py` `record_observations`, `src/ebay_email_alerts.py` `_detect_listing_type`

The parser is conservative by design: no evidence either way yields
`LISTING_TYPE_UNKNOWN`. `record_observations` excludes auctions by
`listing.is_auction`, i.e. `listing_type == "auction"` — so an *unknown*
listing is written into the corpus as an asking price. If it was an auction,
what got written is a **current bid**, which this project's non-negotiables
say must never become a comp.

The corpus did not record `listing_type`, so the leak could not be measured
after the fact. It has the right shape to be real: several `exact` buckets
hold one $0.99–$1.00 row against a $13–$15 median of the same card.

**Done:** `listing_type` is now stored on every observation, so this becomes
measurable from next run, and the run warns when 40%+ of listings arrive
with an unreadable format.

**Not done, and deliberately:** excluding unknown-type listings outright
would gut the corpus on an unvalidated assumption. Decide it with the data
the new field produces.

### H2. One product under two names is two half-depth buckets — FIXED
**Where:** `src/card_identity.py` `SET_ALIASES`, `BARE_SET_BY_MANUFACTURER`

- `"Optic"` (40 rows) and `"Donruss Optic"` (112 rows) were separate
  buckets. No other manufacturer makes an Optic line.
- `"Sapphire"` resolved against the *extracted manufacturer*, which is the
  **leftmost** brand word — and sellers routinely put the parent brand
  first. `"Topps 2024 Bowman Sapphire Colson Montgomery #BCP-8"` is a Bowman
  card whose manufacturer resolves to Topps. Twelve of the fourteen
  bare-Sapphire rows in the corpus are Bowman, several titled exactly that
  way.

**Fix:** `BARE_SET_BY_BRAND` resolves against the brand words *present in
the title*, Bowman first (Bowman is a Topps line, so a title naming both is
a Bowman card). `Optic` became a flat alias. Depth is the scarcest resource
this project has, and these were splitting it for free.

### H3. Named refractor parallels were being discarded — FIXED
**Where:** `src/card_identity.py` `UNAMBIGUOUS_PARALLELS`

Seven parallel names appearing directly in front of "Refractor"/"Prizm" in
the corpus were not in the vocabulary, so a scarce named parallel keyed the
same bucket as the base refractor: Shock (44 rows), Reptilian (25),
Geometric (11), Lazer (7), Pandora (3), Velocity (2), Tri-Color (2). One
Caleb Wilson `exact` bucket held ten Reptilian Refractors and two Geometric
ones under the single name "Refractor".

**Left out on purpose** despite appearing in the same position: `Mosaic` and
`Chrome` (set names), `Prism` (a seller's misspelling of Prizm), and
`Flash` / `Pulse` / `Surge` / `Leather`, which are ordinary English as often
as they are parallel names. **These four want a hobby opinion, not a
measurement** — if they are real parallels in the sets you follow, adding
them is a one-line change.

### H4. Promoting `is_base` to a comp key would NOT be safe — QUESTION CLOSED
**Where:** `src/card_identity.py` `_extract_is_base`

The field's docstring left this open and named the test: "replaying a corpus
several weeks wide and confirming that the buckets it creates contain what
they claim to." That test has now been run on the 27-day corpus.

Asserting base as a parallel sentinel creates 88 buckets of three or more
covering 457 observations — and **half of them (44) hold more than one known
card number.** They do not contain what they claim to:

| Bucket | Range | What is in it |
|---|---|---|
| Bedard / 2023 / Upper Deck Series 2 | $2.00 – $210 | Glossy Rookies insert #R47 through Young Guns #451 |
| Bedard / 2023 / Young Guns | $1.00 – $500 | base #451, Canvas #C382, Extended Series Retro |
| Crow-Armstrong / 2024 / Topps Chrome | $0.99 – $799 | base #16, 35th Anniversary #89CB-19, a Logofractor |

The cause is structural, not a loose guard: "base" is a claim about the
**parallel** and says nothing about **which card**. A product issues one
base card per player and a dozen inserts, all unparalleled, all reading as
base. The honest version of this idea is not a base sentinel at
`same_card` — it is better card-number extraction feeding `exact`, which
already keys on it. The finding and its evidence are now recorded at the
field itself so it is not re-litigated.

### H5. `Listing.total_cost` and `economics.Acquisition.total_cost` disagree about tax
**Where:** `src/models.py`, `src/economics.py`, `src/focus.py`, `src/targets.py`

`Listing.total_cost` is price + shipping. `Acquisition.total_cost` is price
+ shipping + sales tax. `focus._price` and `targets.best_hit` use the first;
the deal gate uses the second. At the shipped `sales_tax_pct: 0.0` they are
identical, so this is latent — **set a real tax rate and your $40 focus
ceiling and your target buy zones silently become pre-tax numbers while the
deal maths is post-tax.** Not fixed: the honest fix threads the rate into
`focus` and `targets`, which is a wider change than this pass warranted.

## MEDIUM

### M1. `logs/scraper.log` was committed once
`.gitignore` covers it now (with a comment explaining why the pattern has no
trailing comment). The file carries every listing title and price a run saw
plus the report recipient. It is out of the working tree but **still in git
history** — worth knowing, not worth rewriting history over for a personal
repo.

### M2. Dead code, measured
Defined in `src/`, reachable only from their own module, their tests, or a
`--legacy` script flag; never from the pipeline:

| Symbol | File | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `build_comp_table`, `compute_median`, `CompStats` | `comps.py` | legacy engine |
| `build_hierarchical_comp_table`, `lookup_hierarchical_comp` | `comps.py` | legacy engine; used by `replay_corpus --legacy` |
| `COMP_LEVELS`, `CONFIDENCE_BY_LEVEL`, `_exact_key`, `_near_exact_key`, `_family_key` | `comps.py` | legacy engine |
| `as_buckets` | `price_history.py` | **genuinely dead** — tests only |
| `breakeven_grade_probability` | `economics.py` | tests only; a documented unshipped feature |
| `detect_grading` | `matcher.py` | a compatibility wrapper with no remaining src caller |

~250 lines. The legacy engine earns its keep as the before/after evidence
`replay_corpus --legacy` prints; `as_buckets` does not.

### M3. `fetch_ebay_active` has no pagination, retries, backoff or rate limiting
**Where:** `src/ebay_client.py`

One request per player, `limit` capped at 50, a non-200 logged and swallowed
as `[]`. Dormant (the developer account was declined), so this is a
correctness debt rather than a live one — but if the API is ever granted,
`[]` on a 500 is the "quiet day that never happened" pattern this project
fixed everywhere else. `search_sold_items` has the same shape.

### M4. `counters["titles_seen"]` still counts per-email
The cross-email merge (C4) happens after `extract_listings_from_html` has
incremented its counters, so an item in two emails is counted twice in the
truncation-rate denominator. The *rate* is close to right; the absolute
count is not.

### M5. Corpus file growth
`data/ebay_alert_price_history.json` is 2.3 MB at 27 days and is rewritten
and committed every run. At the 180-day retention window and the current
~250 rows/day it lands near 15 MB of pretty-printed JSON re-committed daily.
Git deltas it well for now (950 KiB pack). The lever is
`price_history_max_age_days` — at `half_life_days: 30` an observation at day
180 already carries under 2% of a fresh one's weight, so 90 days would cost
almost nothing and halve the file. Storage format is not the problem;
inspectability is worth the bytes.

### M6. Long functions
`report.classify_sections` (181 lines), `main.evaluate_listings` (237),
`main.run` (184). Each is a straight-line sequence of gates with a comment
per gate, which is the readable shape for this kind of code — but
`evaluate_listings` is now doing identity counting, gate evaluation, comp
lookup, economics and auction handling in one pass. Splitting the auction
branch out is the one that would pay.

## What was NOT wrong

Worth saying, because an audit that only lists faults misrepresents the
codebase:

- **Secrets.** No credential in the repo, `.env` ignored, no password
  reaches a log, the failure email carries a traceback (which has no locals)
  and nothing else. Email subjects are built from counts and dates, and
  `EmailMessage` raises on CR/LF anyway — no header injection. Every
  seller-authored string reaching the HTML is `html.escape`d.
- **Failure observability.** `reasons.py` + `observability.py` are better
  than most production systems: every listing exits with exactly one reason,
  an unknown reason string raises, and `unexplained_count()` asserts the
  invariant in the email itself.
- **State-write ordering** in `main.run` — corpus before the send, dedupe
  and run marker after — is correct and correctly explained. So is
  `CorruptCorpus` refusing to overwrite six months of data with one day's.
- **The model/renderer split.** `build_model` decides, `render_text` and
  `report_html.render` only typeset. This is what makes "whichever half of
  the email you read is the same report" structural rather than hopeful.
- **The economics module.** No rounding, no clamping, every assumption
  carried as a printable sentence next to the number it produced.

## Follow-up, 2026-09-23: measured against real alert HTML

Six real alert emails were run through the parser (read via Gmail, not
committed -- they carry account tracking ids). Four findings, all fixed:

| # | Finding | Measured | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Search matches dropped for a cut-off surname.** eBay cuts a match's title to ~30 characters; the player check needs the full name. | **64 of 79** search matches discarded as `NO_PLAYER_MATCH`; 0 of 42 recommendations. The corpus and the report were mostly eBay's picks. | Player taken from the alert's own saved-search keywords when they name exactly one watchlist player. Shown in the report, never recorded as a comp (a "psa" match whose title lost "PSA" would land in the raw bucket). |
| F2 | **Opening bids recorded as asking prices.** The template prints "N bids" only once somebody has bid; an untouched auction is indistinguishable from Buy It Now. | $0.99 (183 rows) and $1.00 (86) spike far above neighbours. 5 of 9 would-flags on 2026-09-22 were $0.99/$1.00 rows at "93% under". | Unreadable format at <= $1.00 is treated as an auction; existing rows dropped at load (298). Would-flags now 8, none under $4. A saved search filtered to Buy It Now (`LH_BIN=1`) or Auction (`LH_Auction=1`) now labels every match it returns. |
| F3 | **The cron was scheduled before the alerts arrived.** | Alerts land 13:55-14:25 UTC daily; the cron said 13:00. **Corrected 09-24:** GitHub actually started it between 16:40 and 18:30 UTC every day checked (09-08..22), after the burst, so reports were not in fact a day stale -- the original claim here was wrong. | Cron set to 14:30 / 18:30 UTC: right after the burst if GitHub is on time, no later than before if it is not. (An interim 15:00 would have pushed the email to ~19:00-20:30.) |
| F4 | **The 40%-unknown-format alarm fired every run.** The template has no "Buy It Now" label at all, so unknown is the normal state for fixed-price. | ~80% unknown on a healthy email. | Replaced with the signal a template change would actually produce: 50+ listings and not one auction. |

Also: the photo alt's `Image of ` prefix is stripped from titles, recommendations are
tagged (`is_recommendation`) and counted in the log, and `price_history_max_age_days`
is 90 (M5).

**2026-09-23, later.** Two follow-ups on the same evidence:

- A search match's full title is known to contain every positive keyword of
  its search. `desirability.search_implied` reads "auto" and a print-run
  group like `(/99,/50,/25)` as attributes of the card, so a cut-off title
  from a numbered or auto search still ranks and passes the /99 ceiling. The
  report labels these `(PER SEARCH)`. A grade is never inferred ("psa" also
  matches "PSA 10 candidate" on raw cards), and nothing inferred reaches the
  comp corpus.
- Alerts show at most 12-16 of a search's new matches. On the six measured,
  five searches were capped (e.g. 157 new, 16 shown). The footer now names
  every capped search, worst first, so a too-broad search is visible the
  morning it happens.
